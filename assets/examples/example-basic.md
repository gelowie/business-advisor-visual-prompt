# Example — Basic problem (T1)

Illustrative: a fictional business. Research lines show the format only; in a real run a sourced figure carries a real source and date.

## What the user said

> my customers keep complaining and i dont know what to do anymore its getting worse

## Intake

Provisional pick: T1 (vague, no domain named). Nothing beyond "complaints" was known, so one batch of four questions was asked: what kind of business and industry (combined); what customers complain about; how long, and what changed around then; what has been tried.

Answers: a small café; the complaints are mixed (some about waits, some about drinks) and the user cannot name a pattern; about three months, since two new staff were hired; talked to the staff informally, nothing changed. The user added that the reviews are now hurting new customer traffic, and that success means fewer complaints and better reviews. The answers did not settle on one domain, so T1 stands. A café plainly maps to local brick-and-mortar, so no assumption is listed.

## Research

Two queries on rising complaints after hiring in small food businesses, with no names or figures in them. No figure passed the filter (no named source and date for a number), so only a qualitative pattern is kept.

## Delivered output

## 📋 Business Visual Prompt

**Topic:** Rising customer complaints at a small café
**Problem:** Complaints have grown over about three months, since two new staff were hired, with no pattern the owner can name, and the reviews are deterring new customers.
**Task:** Find the likely cause and what to fix first.
**Template:** T1 Basic
**Target:** business-advisor-pro

### Prompt to send

```text
In my words:
"my customers keep complaining and i dont know what to do anymore its getting worse"

Business:
- Type: local brick-and-mortar
- Industry: café (food and beverage)

Known (please don't re-ask):
- Complaints are mixed (some about waits, some about drinks) and I cannot name a pattern; they also show up in reviews
- It started about three months ago, after two new staff were hired
- I spoke to the staff informally and nothing changed
- Success means fewer complaints and better reviews
- It is urgent: the reviews are hurting new customer traffic

Research context (unverified unless sourced):
- Pattern (unverified): complaints that rise after new hires often trace to gaps in onboarding and unclear roles at peak hours; the same picture can also come from a change in volume or product quality.

Outcome I want:
Find the most likely root cause and what to do first. If the cause cannot be settled from what I have given, tell me which one or two facts would settle it.
```

### Why this structure

The problem is single and contained, so the prompt stays short. It gives the advisor the user's own words, what has been tried and what success means, and asks for cause and first move rather than a full plan.
