# Intake Checklist and Question Bank

Used in Phase 1. The goal is to know what the user said and ask only for what is still missing. Nothing here is a script to read out.

## How to use this file

1. Read the user's message and sort it into **Known** and **Not known** using the checklist below.
2. Make a **provisional template pick** (`references/research-guide.md`, section 7). If the facts are too thin, use T1. The pick stays provisional until the answers arrive.
3. Look up that template's **priority order** below. Ask only for **Not known** items that would change the prompt, in one batch, at most 4 questions, highest priority first.
4. Related figures may share one question (see Combined questions). Never ask what is already answered. Never drip-feed questions across several turns.
5. Finalize the template from the answers. A second batch is allowed only when an answer reveals a different problem type or contradicts an earlier fact.
6. The user may answer partially, say they do not know, or ask you to proceed. Record each unanswered item as **Not provided**, accept a labeled range ("roughly X to Y, my estimate"), and continue. Never invent a figure.

## Core checklist (what to know, not what to ask)

| # | Fact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business type | One of: service-based, digital product/SaaS, physical product/e-commerce, local brick-and-mortar. Plainly settled by the user's words, it is mapped directly; left open, it is an assumption (see below). |
| 2 | Industry | Free text. |
| 3 | The exact problem | In the user's own words. Keep the original wording for the prompt. |
| 4 | How long it has been happening | A duration, or "since [event]". |
| 5 | What has been tried and what happened | Include anything that made it worse. |
| 6 | What success looks like | The outcome the user wants, in their terms. |
| 7 | Urgency | No deadline, or a deadline or runway. A dated deadline or runway of days or weeks is a hard clock (see T9). |
| 8 | Jurisdiction | Country and state or province. Needed only when the problem touches law, tax, employment, privacy or fundraising. If it is relevant and unknown, include it in the batch of questions. Otherwise leave it out. |

## Stated, inferred, assumed

- **Stated:** the user said it. It may go under *Known*.
- **Inferred and material:** the user did not say it, but the prompt depends on it (for example, a stage, a currency, whether a freelancer was a contractor or an employee, or a business type their words leave open). It goes under *Assumptions to verify*, never under *Known*. If it is cheap to ask and sits in the top priorities, ask instead.
- **Mapping the user's words to a business type** is classification, not an assumption, when the words plainly settle it (an online store, a café, a marketing agency, a software subscription). It is an assumption when they leave it open (for example, a shop that might also sell online).
- **Inferred and immaterial:** leave it out.

## Priority order by template

Ask in this order, skipping what is known. Stop at 4 questions. Items joined by "+" can share one question.

| Template | Ask in this order |
|---|---|
| T1 Basic | 1. What the problem looks like in practice. 2. Business type + industry. 3. How long + what changed around then. 4. What has been tried. |
| T2 Operational | 1. Where the process breaks (start, middle, end). 2. People involved and their roles. 3. One-off or repeating. 4. Existing SOP or written process. |
| T3 Strategic | 1. The options being weighed. 2. Decision deadline. 3. What happens if the user chooses wrong or waits. 4. Who else has a say. |
| T4 Financial | 1. Combined numbers: currency + period + cash balance now + revenue and expenses for the period (or net burn). 2. How customers pay + how late. 3. Debt or investor obligations. 4. What changed recently + problem type (cash flow, profitability, pricing, funding). |
| T5 Marketing | 1. Main symptom. 2. Target audience. 3. Channels in use + monthly budget. 4. Conversion or lead volume. |
| T6 People | 1. Who is affected + nature of the problem. 2. What has been said or done already. 3. Team size. 4. Jurisdiction, when discipline, dismissal or pay is involved. |
| T7 Legal | 1. Jurisdiction (always). 2. Type of issue + parties involved. 3. Current status + deadline or notice. 4. Documents involved + whether a lawyer is involved. |
| T8 Complex | 1. The list of problems. 2. Which problems the user thinks cause which. 3. What has been tried on each. 4. Budget + timeline + resources. |
| T9 Crisis | 1. The hard clock (deadline, runway or notice). 2. What happened and when. 3. Who already knows. 4. Resources available now. |
| T10 Growth | 1. The growth move. 2. What blocks growth. 3. Numbers: revenue per period + currency + team size. 4. Budget + timeline; add jurisdiction when entering a new country, hiring abroad or raising money. |

When more than 4 items are missing, the lower-priority items go to *Not provided yet*. Do not ask them in a second batch unless the second-batch rule applies.

## Combined questions

One question may gather several related items when the user can answer them in one line. Examples:

- Numbers (T4, T10): "In any format: the currency and period, your cash balance now, and revenue and expenses for that period (or your monthly net burn)."
- Marketing (T5): "Which channels bring leads today, and roughly what do you spend on them per month?"
- Context (T1): "What kind of business is it, and what is the industry?"

Business type and industry may be added to the first question of any template when neither is stated nor plainly settled by the user's words.

A combined question counts as one of the 4. Never combine items from different problem types.

## Follow-up questions by problem type

Pick at most what fits the gap. Skip any whose answer is already known.

**Operations and workflow**
- Does it break at the start, in the middle or at the end?
- Is it a one-time breakdown or does it repeat?
- Is there an existing SOP or written process?

**Financial**
- Is this a cash flow, profitability, pricing or funding problem?
- How do customers pay, and how often do they pay late?
- Has anything changed recently: pricing, headcount, a new customer, a new cost, the season?

**Marketing and sales**
- Are customers leaving, not arriving, or both?
- Which channels bring leads today?

**People and HR**
- Is this about one person, a team or the whole culture?
- Have there already been direct conversations?

**Strategic decision**
- What are the options?
- What is the cost of waiting?
- Who else has a say?

**Legal and compliance**
- Contract, IP, regulation or dispute?
- Is the risk current or did it already happen?
- Is a lawyer already involved?

**Growth and expansion**
- What is driving the push to grow?
- What has stopped growth until now?

**Crisis**
- What triggered it, and when?
- Who is affected?
- What is the worst case if nothing changes this week?

**Several problems**
- Which one, if solved, would change the others?
- Does any problem cause another, or are they independent?
- Which one matters most right now? (ask this when the user names several independent problems and no priority)

## Tone

- Conversational and professional.
- Present the batch as one short message, not an interrogation.
- If the user is rushing, ask the single most important question or none, and build with what exists.
